# Training

## **The Transform phase**

This is the part of the change management process where the Transform phase begins.

## **Why is training important?**

- Establishes the 'why'

  Helps people understand how the change will be beneficial to their roles and the organization.
- Imparts skills for success

  Gives people the skills they need to be successful in making the change.
- Promotes engagement

  Encourages employees to invest in the change.
- Minimizes productivity issues

  Provides a roadmap to successfully navigate disruption.

## **Create a training outline and plan**

A training analysis and plan should consist of the following:

- Outline training goals, objectives, and success criteria.
- Develop a training outline that includes all stages of the training.
- Plan logistical training considerations, such as:
  - Instructor-led training, elearning, or both
  - Group training, individual training, or both
  - Pre-work training, on-the-job training, or both
- Training evaluation considerations.

Evaluate existing content (formats that exist, content gaps, leveraging possibilities, accuracy, how engaging the existing formats are, if feedback from previous iterations was implemented, and so on).

Think about how to leverage prior knowledge and build upon it to train learners on the change. It’s likely that learners have prior knowledge to draw on to understand the new information that's introduced in the training.

- **Needs Assessment:**
  - Understand and document the specific training needs for each user group across the organization.
  - Identify distinct user groups (for example IT Administrators, Mobile Users, Customer Service Reps).
  - Define key learning objectives and specific tools for each group.
  - Determine the best delivery approach for each group.
- **Curriculum:**
  - Create a comprehensive inventory of all training materials and sessions.
  - Document the title and description for each training session.
  - Assign trainers, delivery methods (in-person, online), and languages.
  - Track training sessions and attendance to measure completion.

## **Success metrics**

Key performance indicators to measure platform adoption.

1. **Active Gemini Enterprise Users**

    The number or percentage of targeted employees who interact with Gemini Enterprise a certain amount of time within a specific timeframe (such as weekly or monthly). This is a fundamental adoption metric. A high level indicates that employees are not just aware of the agents but are actively incorporating them into their workflows.
2. **Task Automation Rate**

    The percentage of a specific task or process that is successfully completed by an AI agent without requiring human intervention. This KPI directly measures the core purpose of most agents: automation.
3. **Cycle Time Reduction**

    The average reduction in the time it takes to complete a process from start to finish, thanks to the involvement of Gemini Enterprise. This metric quantifies the efficiency gains from using Gemini Enterprise such as faster time to resolution of a customer query or reduced time spent reviewing documentation.
4. **Agent Development and Deployment Velocity**

    The number of new agents successfully built and deployed into production over a certain period (for example per quarter). This KPI gauges the organization's ability to leverage the Gemini Enterprise platform to solve new problems. A healthy velocity indicates that teams are identifying valuable use cases, are proficient with the development tools, and have an effective governance process.

## **Best practices**

Follow these best practices to finalize a training outline and plan:

- **Help users be productive on Day 1:**
  - Enable users to be productive from Day-1 with simple guidance on setup.
  - Focus on value (not features) by tying training to relevant practices or use cases.
- **Incorporate targeted training needed for special user groups:**
  - Address the user's needs in the training plan.
  - Identify advanced product features that would bring the most value to certain user groups, and plan training sessions to highlight these features.
- **Use existing training resources and formats:**
  - Work with the company’s training or HR department to understand how training is traditionally delivered in the organization.
  - If there is an online learning system and most users take eLearning courses, that may be a great option.
- **Ensure that the training format fits the content:**
  - Most users prefer to learn more complicated training from a live instructor so they can ask questions.
  - Conduct in-depth product training customized to the needs of each user group and using diversified training vehicles.

## **Plan Training**

There are eight different steps involved in planning a successful training rollout.

1. **Identify user groups**

    Identify and outline key user groups within the customer’s organization.
2. **Identify required skills**

    Identify the skills/knowledge they will require to be successful after their go-live.
3. **Outline requirements**

    Outline the courses required, delivery methods, duration, learning objectives, and targeted audience.
4. **Create resources**

    Create or identify the required training resources.
5. **Create a training plan**

    Create a detailed training plan for the intended audience.
6. **Create a record of activities**

    Create a record of the training activities and track user participation.
7. **Assess abilities**

    Assess users' abilities before and after completing the training.
8. **Amend training**

    Amend the training plan to include advanced product and process training.
