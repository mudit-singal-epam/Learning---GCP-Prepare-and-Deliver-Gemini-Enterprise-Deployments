# Sponsorship and Engagement


## **The Excite phase**

This is the part of the change management process where the Excite phase begins.

## **Why is sponsorship important?**

There are some important factors that sponsorship provides to help ensure the ongoing use of Gemini Enterprise long after it has been deployed.

- **Identifies the right people for success**

  Ensures that you’ve thought about everyone who needs to be included for success to happen.
- **Establishes engagement**

  Engagement of key users in the organization is the most critical success factor of any change.
- **Widens the circle of support**

  Involving key sponsors extends support, mitigates issues, and builds advocacy.
- **Ensures that change continues**

  Engaged sponsors ensure the ongoing success of the change.

## **Stakeholder responsibilities**

There are some core responsibilities that the project stakeholder must own, and it is a good idea to make sure they are clear about their responsibilities from the start. The stakeholder needs to:

- Provide a fully engaged sponsor and project team, including a dedicated project manager and subject matter experts.

- Ensure all necessary system access, networking, and connectivity are established prior to kickoff.

- Provide a Google Cloud project allowlisted for Gemini Enterprise with required APIs enabled.

- Engage the appropriate SMEs and end users to publicize the out of the box features to their audience.

- Deliver a golden dataset with search queries, expected responses, and citations for evaluation and prototyping.

- Identify and provide the list of data sources and relevant attributes to be integrated.

- Actively participate in discovery, implementation, and testing phases, including timely decision-making and feedback.

- Ensure data for BigQuery or Google Cloud Storage connectors is readily available in a Google Cloud project with appropriate access.

- Review and approve technical requirements for all connectors and grant necessary permissions to Gemini Enterprise.

## **Key stakeholders to identify**

In order to fulfil all responsibilities efficiently the project stakeholder must identify some key stakeholders in the organization to perform certain roles to enable a successful delivery of Gemini Enterprise. Let's take some time to explore the key stakeholder types that you should be bringing onboard.

- **Executive Sponsor:**

  - **Decision-maker**.

  - The change leader who authorizes the change and mobilizes people.

- **Executive Committee:**

  - **Influencers**.

  - Business leaders and influencers providing department support.

- **Early Adopters:**

  - **Change agents**.

  - Early adopters who help speed up change efforts in groups.

- **Communicators and Trainers:**

  - **Transformers**.

  - Innovative thinkers who continue to drive transformation after go-live.

- **Security and Networking:**

  - **Security**.

  - To ensure security, provide access and prevent last-minute blockers.

## **Executive Sponsor**

As part of the Excite phase of the Sponsorship and Engagement work stream, you’ll identify an Executive Sponsor. The Executive Sponsor role is a critical decision-making role, and the one who authorizes the change to occur within the organization.

- This change leader authorizes the change, ties it to business strategy, and mobilizes people to take action.
- This individual sends the initial announcement, shares the vision, secures support and resources, socializes change, and addresses roadblocks.
- To indicate that this is a business change, this person should ideally be a recognizable figure in the business, such as the CEO, or sit in the C-Suite, such as the COO.

### **Active and visible executive sponsorship is critical**

When asked to identify key contributors to the success of their change initiatives, participants in a research study placed active and visible sponsorship at the top of the list.

> [!NOTE]
> No Executive Sponsor = No Go.
>
> A clear executive sponsor and a defined problem statement are the #1 predictors of success.

### Timeline

#### Executive Sponsor: Key attributes

- The executive sponsor should be a senior member of the organization.
- It will help if they are well known, respected, and influential.
- It is just as important that they are able to commit to being actively involved. Many senior executives are already maxed out on their time commitments, so you need to ensure that you attract the right sponsor, and get them to commit time to the project.
- The executive sponsor must share the vision because they set the culture and tone for the project. They should be able to give the elevator pitch - a one or two minute synopsis of what will be achieved.

#### Executive Sponsor: Approach

- The executive sponsor is also the face of the project to the organization.
- They announce the change and secure support for it by driving cultural change within the organization and addressing the roadblocks.
- Not everyone in the senior leadership team will have the level of detail on the project as the sponsor; some of them might even be resistant.
- It is the job of the sponsor to align the leadership and to address resistance.
- The executive sponsor must place a lot of trust in the deployment team by allocating resources to the project.
- When you repay that trust by successfully delivering change, the executive sponsor gets to lead the celebration of the success.

#### Redundancy in leadership

In addition to managing the planned changes, you should also consider unplanned changes, such as the departure of key personnel. Redundancy is imperative, especially within project leadership.

You should ask the question: Who would have the vision and authority to carry on the project in the absence of the executive sponsor?

In this way, even if the executive sponsor departs the organization, the change continues uninterrupted because the organization still has continuity of the vision at the leadership level.

A member from the Executive committee would be a great candidate to act as Executive Sponsor moving forward.

### **Conduct executive briefings**

An executive briefing is the first key milestone in deploying the change.

At the executive briefing, you should introduce Google’s approach to rolling out the change.

Everyone involved in the project, including salespeople and project managers, should help to promote change management to the executives during the sales cycle.

They should also help them understand what is needed from the organization to successfully deploy and support Gemini Enterprise.

> [!NOTE]
> Tips for conducting successful executive briefings
>
> The executive briefing introduces prospects to the Google methodology and inspires them.
>
> - Walk through the deployment experience.
> - Explain the proven methodology and approach.
> - Share success stories and targeted use cases.
> - Provide inspiration about positive change.
> - Address common concerns and issues regarding people and change.

## **Engage leaders for go-live support and promotion**

Solicit broad support from executives and leadership by recruiting a cross-functional executive committee that can:

- Provide feedback on impacts and messaging
- Identify and address roadblocks
- Communicate key messages to the team
- And address resistance early and often

### Timeline

#### Executive committee: Key attributes

The executive committee is composed of business leaders and influencers providing department-specific support and leadership.

An executive committee plays a liaison role within the business relating to matters such as:

- Two-way team communications
- Leading by example
- Thinking long term

#### Executive committee: Two-way team communications

As part of two-way team communications, the executive committee:

- Communicates the vision and benefits for the team
- Shares messages and successes within department
- Addresses departmental questions and concerns
- Raises risks and issues to project team

#### Executive committee: Lead by example

As part of leading by example, the Executive Committee:

- Uses Gemini Enterprise and encourages teams to use the tools
- Encourages teams to participate in training and continue to build skills
- Supports peer-to-peer coaching and the Early Adopters program

#### Executive committee: Think long-term

As part of thinking long-term, the Executive Committee:

- Helps uncover use cases and success stories within departments
- Maintains energy around the change until it’s business as usual

## **Recruit and train Early Adopters**

It's important to recruit the right people as Early Adopters, and in the right number.

- Aim to recruit a range of people in any client organization to act as Early Adopters.
- This involves:
  - Proactively recruiting Early Adopters and engaging with them.
  - Keeping Early Adopters visible and engaged throughout the deployment process.

### Timeline

#### Early Adopters: Key attributes

Early Adopters are ambassadors for change within the business.

- Typically they are early adopters who will help scale and accelerate change efforts in cross-functional business groups.
- They are enthusiastic about the transformation and about gaining business value from it.
- They do not need to be technical people; their role will be more about providing leadership on the ground in the business.

#### Early Adopters: Approach

Early Adopters help to transform processes and support the co-development of solutions that support the change.

- Gemini Enterprise is a no-code development platform that lets anyone without coding experience build mobile and web applications.

## **Communicators and Trainers**

It can be difficult to sustain innovation and change. People revert to old ways of doing things, commitment can waver as people move on to other projects, and, of course, everyone is very busy in their day jobs and often do not have the time to identify and lead initiatives.

- This is where Communicators and Trainers can help.
- This is a group of people drawn from different business units who are the biggest supporters of change.
- Communicators and Trainers keep the momentum going in the change process.

### Timeline

#### Communicators and Trainers: Approach

Some of the tasks Communicators and Trainers do, include:

- Facilitating Transformation Labs
- Sharing success stories across the organization
- Ensuring that leaders are engaged and lead by example
- Ensuring employees have access to ongoing education
- Measuring user adoption and impact

Communicators and Trainers also prepare for new feature launches, to refresh and maintain end-user resources for ongoing adoption and to support new hire learning initiatives.

## **Security and Networking**

You should bring Security in on Day 1. Engage the customer's SecOps and Governance teams from the very first meeting to prevent last-minute blockers. Ensure you have relevant security and networking personnel onboard from the start to ensure security and access to the Gemini Enterprise solution. For an enterprise-wide rollout, the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) or equivalent should be engaged.

Security and networking will need to establish the foundational identity and organizational structure in Google Cloud, including setting up super admins, users, and groups, and creating the Google Cloud domain and organization root. They may also need to configure Google Cloud's Workforce Identity Federation to securely connect the project stakeholder’s existing external identity provider (IdP) with Google Cloud. This requires collaborating with:

- Cloud Administrators
- Cloud Networking Specialists (verifying DNS records of project stakeholder’s domain)
- Infrastructure Architects
- Accounting team personnel who hold business credit cards (to temporarily pay the setup if the offline billing account has not yet been setup)
- Identity & Access Management (IAM) Leads.

### Timeline

#### Security and Networking: Approach

Some of the tasks **Security and Networking** do, include:

- Establishing a Google Cloud Identity tenant integration, including importing user groups.
- Verifying the primary domain within Google Cloud.
- Creating a Google Cloud domain to ensure domain record maintenance for validation within two business days.
- Setting up all superadmins, users, and groups within Google Cloud Identity.
- Creating the organization root in Google Cloud.

For **Workforce Identity Federation (if applicable)**:

- Gathering necessary information for IdP integration.
- Configuring Google Cloud's Workforce Identity Federation to connect to the external IdP.
- Verifying streamlined user authentication and authorization.

## **General delivery and engagement best practices**

Successful Gemini Enterprise engagements benefit from strong project management, clear communication, and robust evaluation practices. So let's take a moment to explore what works well.

- **Daily stand-ups:**

  - Implementing concise (for example 15-minute) daily stand-ups with project stakeholders can significantly improve progress visibility and alignment, particularly on accelerated project timelines.

- **Choosing a broader audience for Day-1 value experience:**

  - Share the Day-1 value experience with end users which accelerates the collaboration between Account team and Project Stakeholders to find potential use cases that are repetitive and manual where these out-of-the-box features can assist.

- **Blended Search demonstrations:**

  - Project stakeholders are often impressed by Gemini Enterprise's "Blended Search" capabilities, which allow users to ask questions that cross-reference information from multiple connected data sources to provide a comprehensive response.

  - Popular and effective combinations for demonstration include:.

  - - Google Drive, Calendar, and People (for first-party Workspace productivity scenarios).

  - - Jira, Github, and Confluence (for developer-focused information retrieval).

  - - ServiceNow, Workday, and Confluence (for finding information and initiating HR or IT-related tasks).

- **Two-stage evaluation:**

  - A phased approach to evaluating search result quality has proven effective:.

  - **Automated Quantitative Benchmarking**: Initially, use tools like Colab notebooks to run automated evaluations against a predefined "golden dataset" of questions and expected answers.

  - This helps establish baseline performance and tune system instructions or search controls.

  - **Qualitative User Acceptance Testing (UAT)**: Once the application is stable and tuned, open it up to a wider group of business users with a structured set of test questions to gather qualitative feedback on relevance, accuracy, and overall user experience.

- **Early Golden Dataset preparation:**

  - Encourage project stakeholders to start preparing their "Golden Dataset" for evaluation as early as possible to ensure ample time for thoughtful input before the evaluation phase begins.

- **Integrated Security discussions:**

  - Incorporate security considerations into the onboarding process.

  - Holding dedicated security workshops early can prevent downstream delays by proactively addressing connector permissions, identity management, and data governance with project stakeholder security teams.
