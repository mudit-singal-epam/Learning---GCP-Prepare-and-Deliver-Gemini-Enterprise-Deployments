# Organizational Analysis

Organizational Analysis helps an organization to understand the impact of the change on end users and illustrates how this change is a positive transformation to the way their users work.

- **Identify user groups**

  Identify the groups affected by the change and the support each group needs.
- **Pinpoint skills required**

  Determine the skills and knowledge people will require to be successful after their go-live.
- **Determine change requirements**

  Identify organizational policies or processes that need to be changed.
- **Define transformation areas**

  Identify business processes and solutions that could be transformed using Gemini Enterprise.

## **Assess organizational impacts and document cultural goals**

As part of the Organizational Analysis work stream, your customer will also assess organizational impacts and document cultural goals, for example:

- Consider positive and challenging impacts.

- Identify success metrics for the change.

- Document cultural goals.

> [!NOTE]
> Organizational impacts can be positive, but they can also present challenges.
>
> Positive impacts include:
> - Opportunities for innovation
> - Increased operational efficiency from existing employees
> - Improved employee morale
>
> Challenging impacts include:
> - Training time needed
> - Concerns around job security
> - Inconsistent adoption of (or adaptation to) the change

## **Objections are your friends**

Whatever the reason for objections, remember to welcome them, as they provide an opportunity to better understand the position of the prospect and get more specific information about their current setup.

In fact, if a prospect has no objections, this can often be a sign of low commitment or engagement. So, when there are objections, they can be used to advance the sale by getting more specific information about the prospect’s current environment.

- In the case of a lack of knowledge, you can inform the customer of what you think they need to know.
- If they don’t believe the advantages of Gemini Enterprise, you should endeavor to prove those advantages to them.
- If they raise a specific disadvantage or concern about the product, you must demonstrate how Gemini Enterprise can add value to their business and transform their work.

## **Be aware of common pitfalls**

In addition to handling objections, there are a few common pitfalls you can encounter if you’re not careful.

- **Thinking you can preempt or prevent objections:**

  - It’s a mistake to think you can prevent or preempt objections.

  - Preparation is important, and it’s always a good idea to know some of the most common objections and how to handle them before approaching a sale.

  - But by attempting to second-guess the customer you can come across as presumptuous and unwilling to listen.

  - You may inadvertently introduce the customer to a new set of objections which they didn’t have previously.

  - You also run the risk of misjudging their objections - for example, they may appear to be objecting to one aspect of implementing Gemini Enterprise, but in fact are concerned about something else, and you will only discover this by asking them questions and listening to the responses.

- **Minimizing the problem:**

  - Another pitfall is minimizing the problem or trying to convince the customer that their objection isn’t important.

  - If they stress that something is important to them and their business, you should take them at their word.

- **Losing sight of the goal:**

  - Be wary of losing sight of the goal.

  - If a customer has several objections to adopting Gemini Enterprise, try to address all of them in a concise way instead of going into too much detail about a single aspect of Gemini Enterprise implementations.

- **Rushing straight to the answer:**

  - Don’t rush straight to the answer.

  - When handling objections, you don’t want to overwhelm the customer, even if you already know the answer.

  - You should try to tell a story and respond in a conversational way; otherwise, you may appear dismissive and put the customer off.

## **Innovation matrix**

So, what's the most efficient way to identify Gemini Enterprise use cases in an organization and determine which ones to tackle? One recommended method that your customers can use to map new technologies to business needs is the Innovation Matrix.

A simple innovation matrix just requires the customer to come up with four business priorities which can be mapped across a grid against the key capabilities of the new technology. The task is then to think of 3-5 “sticky note” ideas for how you can solve the business needs with these capabilities.

The example below shows an innovation matrix with four business needs mapped across the top: automate proposals, gather documents prior to a case, automate standups, and improve editorial reviews. The Gemini Enterprise capabilities are listed on the left side: Gemini Enterprise queries, no-code agents, Conversational agents, and Agent Development Kit agents.

Encourage your customer to come up with their own business priorities and map them to Gemini Enterprise capabilities. They can utilize the Gemini app for support with generating ideas, however they should always make sure to validate all the ideas to ensure they are feasible and realistic.

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gemini Enterprise queries** | | Gathering documents related to a specific case number could be done in Gemini Enterprise, including case summaries | | |
| **Gemini Enterprise no-code agents** | The structure of document templates could be built into no-code instructions | | | |
| **Conversational agent added to Gemini Enterprise** | | | Each employee chats with an agent, then responses are compiled in an email | |
| **Agent Development Kit (ADK) agent in Gemini Enterprise** | | | Chats could happen through ADK agents as well | ADK agents can read documents and pass on suggestions for a human editor to confirm or build from |

## **Develop a rubric to rate ideas**

Your customer may want to assign points on a 1-5 scale based on categories like:

- **Impact:**

  - How much time would be saved (num of hours * num of people)?.

- **Feasibility:**

  - How certain are you that it will work to the required standard?.

- **Priority:**

  - Does it help the customer move forward on current priority initiatives?.

- **Team readiness:**

  - How ready is the team of users to adopt a new project?.

- **Size of the project:**

  - How much work will this require to implement?.

- **Technical complexity:**

  - Prefer simpler starting use-cases to more complex ones.

## **Select starting projects based on the rubric**

Help your customer plan for their engagement to include some number of prototypes.
For example:

- Explore and iterate on up to 20 query ideas.

- Up to 5 no-code agent ideas.

- 1 ADK or Conversational agent (or save these for separately scoped engagements).
