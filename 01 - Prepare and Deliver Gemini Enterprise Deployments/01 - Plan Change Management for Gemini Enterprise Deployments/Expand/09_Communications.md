# Communications

## **The Expand phase**

This is the part of the change management process where the Expand phase begins.

## **Why is Communications important?**

The work done in the Communications work stream builds awareness, interest, and excitement.

- **Raises awareness**

  It ensures that the project gets visibility, and raises awareness of the change.
- **Build excitement**

  It provides an opportunity to build anticipation and enthusiasm throughout the organization.
- **Sells the benefits**

  It's the chance to show people the benefits of embracing the change.
- **Provides a feedback channel**

  It gives people an opportunity to provide feedback on the change.

## **Multi-directional strategy: Top-down and bottom-up**

Google has a gen AI ecosystem, but the customer will know their business and its needs best. They may be excited about using new technology or completely nervous about integrating it. Either way, let's explore a recommended strategy and approach for thinking about it.

It’s important not to jump into the newest technology headfirst without a plan. This is true for Gemini Enterprise, but also for any new technology.

So let’s start to think about not just how and where to use Gemini Enterprise in a business, but also how the structure of a business needs to change to effectively implement Gemini Enterprise.

It's recommended to take a two-fold, multi-directional approach where your customer simultaneously works top-down and bottom-up. This means combining high-level strategic planning (top-down) with practical input from teams who will use the technology (bottom-up).

- **Top-down:**
  - Create a clear and compelling vision for AI by aligning AI solutions with strategic business priorities.
  - Identify the most important use cases and workflows.
  - Then, consider how AI can support those priorities.
- **Bottom-up:**
  - Encourage teams to share their challenges and ideas for gen AI solutions.
  - Encourage employees to use gen AI at a small scale.
  - Then share their solutions for broader implementation.

Only working top-down could miss so many opportunities.

Sometimes, the best ideas will come from the people who are closest to the problem. It's important to make sure these people can also contribute to the change and adoption.

And that is also why this first top-down approach is important. Firstly, because all these potential mini ideas and experiments can have a place to fit into the greater strategy. Secondly, because they can be experimented with in a way that is safe and responsible for the company.

## **Strategy and roles for expanding use of Gemini Enterprise**

Now, let’s find out how to engage different levels of stakeholders in expanding the usage of Gemini Enterprise. There are two main role categories: **Executives and high-level management**, and **mid-level managers and individual contributors**.

- **Executives and high-level management:**

  Executive sponsorship and a clear vision play critical roles in Gemini Enterprise implementation.

  This is to ensure that Gemini Enterprise initiatives:
  - Align with overall business goals.
  - Receive adequate resources.
  - Gain organization-wide support.
  - Empower employees and teams to experiment.

- **Mid-level managers and individual contributors:**

  Mid-level managers and individual contributors (ICs) have a proximity to daily operations and end-users that will provide invaluable insight.

  They can champion Gemini Enterprise adoption by:
  - Identifying high-impact, feasible solutions that address specific challenges within workflows.
  - Encouraging experimentation and feedback from teams to ensure Gemini Enterprise is effectively integrated into existing processes, fostering increased productivity and innovation.

Successful Gemini Enterprise implementation requires a combination of top-down and bottom-up approaches. For both approaches, it's a good idea to focus on:

- Strategic focus
- Exploration
- Responsible AI
- Resourcing
- Impact
- Continuous improvement

Let’s focus on each of these strategic factors in more detail from the perspective of these key roles.

### Strategic focus

- **Executives and high-level management**
  - Focus on a specific area where Gemini Enterprise can have a significant impact. Once success has been achieved in an initial domain, it can expand to other areas of the business. This focused approach maximizes learning and scalability while ensuring tangible business value.
  - Prioritize use cases that are feasible to implement, actionable, affordable, and have high anticipated business value and return on investment (ROI).
- **Mid-level managers and individual contributors**
  - Identify the most pressing pain points in daily work or those heard directly from customers. These real-world challenges are often the best starting point for finding Gemini Enterprise applications that can have a significant impact.
  - Prioritize understanding user needs, their workflows, and their expectations. A human-centered design approach is essential for ensuring that any solutions meet user needs and deliver a positive experience.
  - Explore use cases in areas of the business that offer a low-risk environment for initial implementation. This allows for valuable learning and experimentation during the initial stages of adoption. It enables teams to refine processes and maximize benefits as confidence grows.

### Exploration

- **Executives and high-level management**
  - Empower employees to experiment with Gemini Enterprise and identify potential use cases. This can involve hosting hackathons or providing dedicated time and resources for exploration.
  - Foster a collaborative environment where employees can share findings, insights, and best practices for using Gemini Enterprise. This leverages the expertise and creativity of individuals across the organization.
- **Mid-level managers and individual contributors**
  - Experiment with different Gemini Enterprise tools and applications. This practical experience will provide valuable insights into the capabilities and limitations of the technology, helping to identify promising use cases.
  - Share findings and results with their teams and colleagues. If initial experiments prove successful, they can consider expanding the scope of their exploration and bringing their insights to the attention of leadership.

### Responsible AI

- **Executives and high-level management**
  - Establish ethical guidelines and implement safety mechanisms to ensure that Gemini Enterprise is used responsibly and securely across the organization.
  - Institute robust data governance practices that support data security and privacy, and follow relevant regulations. Use enterprise tooling like Google Cloud Data Loss Prevention, Cloud Key Management Service, and Chronicle Security Operations to automate data discovery, classification, and de-identification, manage encryption keys, and detect and respond to security threats.
  - Implement a content moderation policy that includes employee review of AI-generated content, particularly in sensitive or high-risk applications.
  - Regularly monitor the performance and impact of Gemini Enterprise to identify and address any unintended consequences or ethical concerns.
- **Mid-level managers and individual contributors**
  - Prioritize responsible AI development and deployment by adhering to company AI standards, aligning efforts with organizational values and mitigating potential risks.
  - Think proactively about potential pitfalls and unintended consequences that might arise from gen AI results when using a Gemini Enterprise implementation.
  - Conduct thorough testing and evaluation to identify and address any biases, safety concerns, or ethical issues.

### Resourcing

- **Executives and high-level management**
  - Create a robust data strategy to ensure their teams can access what they need to get the most out of Gemini Enterprise.
  - Avoid reinventing the wheel by leveraging existing tools and platforms where possible.
  - Invest in developing the necessary talent within the organization by upskilling existing employees and hiring new talent with expertise.
- **Mid-level managers and individual contributors**
  - Leverage the resources readily available within existing teams or departments. This could include existing datasets, tools, or platforms that can be leveraged for Gemini Enterprise.
  - If any ideas or experiments require additional resources, they can speak to leadership and make a case for the potential value and ROI of certain Gemini Enterprise initiatives.

### Impact

- **Executives and high-level management**
  - Set clear goals and define key performance indicators (KPIs) to measure the impact of Gemini Enterprise, like increased sales or reduced customer complaints.
  - Regularly communicate the progress and impact of Gemini Enterprise initiatives to stakeholders, showcasing the value generated and justifying continued investment.
  - Define a messaging strategy to deal with objections around job security, ethical AI, and privacy.
- **Mid-level managers and individual contributors**
  - Demonstrate the tangible impact of Gemini Enterprise experiments to gain support and drive further adoption. Quantify the achievement, whether it's increased efficiency, improved user satisfaction, or cost savings.
  - Articulate how Gemini Enterprise initiatives contribute to the organization's overall business goals and objectives to align efforts with strategic priorities and showcase the value it's delivering.
  - Define and track KPIs that measure the effectiveness of Gemini Enterprise solutions. This data provides evidence of the impact and helps to refine the approach over time.

### Continuous improvement

- **Executives and high-level management**
  - Embrace an iterative development approach, continuously refining Gemini Enterprise solutions based on feedback, performance data, and evolving business needs. 
  - Establish a regular evaluation cadence to assess the quality and effectiveness of Gemini Enterprise. Use this evaluation data to identify areas for improvement and guide future development efforts.
  - Implement mechanisms for gathering feedback from users, stakeholders, and other relevant parties to ensure Gemini Enterprise meets user needs and expectations.
- **Mid-level managers and individual contributors**
  - Perform continuous testing, measurement, and refinement based on user feedback and real-world performance data. These are essential to ensure Gemini Enterprise solutions remain effective and valuable.
  - Update Gemini Enterprise solutions regularly based on new data and feedback to improve performance.
  - Continue Gemini Enterprise education by taking courses, reading articles, and experimenting.

## **Users need a sense of direction**

The rationale or vision for choosing Gemini Enterprise will form the basis of the change effort.

Employees need to understand the reason the organization is choosing Gemini Enterprise:

- What's in it for your organization and employees?
- Why are you making the move to Gemini Enterprise?
- What are your key business objectives?
- What type of transformation are you hoping this change brings to your organization?
- How can Gemini Enterprise improve your team's ways of working?

## **Map out a communications plan**

A communications plan is essential for creating a way to distribute successful solutions and assets, initiate two-way communications channels, gather feedback and run events that harvest the potential of the user's imagination:

- Develop a list of useful queries and distribute them
- Share instructions for no code agents that have been 
found to be useful
- Have a contest for the most useful no-code agents created for each team / department
- Solicit ideas for workflows or documents that could benefit from being guided or automated by more complex agents

## **Share use cases and success stories**

Gathering and sharing successes is key to building support. It's how people understand how this will benefit them. It also gives ideas and confidence to users about how to work in a new way.

Gathering and sharing success stories should be a critical component of communications efforts. Many people create contests or hackathons to gather stories. This phase is a good opportunity to share organic success stories.

- **Share success:**
  - Celebrate successes by actively gathering and sharing success stories.
  - Hold a competition to collect stories.
  - Use Google tools to engage people and crowdsource stories.
  - Provide ongoing, bite-sized tips through email or Google tools.
  - Target stories and use cases to different user groups.
  - Establish a plan for communicating product updates with employees.
- **Demonstrate new ways to work:**
  - In sharing information about the change, it’s important to demonstrate ways to execute it.
  - If adjustments need to be made to how people work, demonstrate tangible ways of making the required changes.
  - Give concrete examples of success stories as a benchmark for what people need to do.
  - Create content to support people through making the change; for example, "Top ten ways to use Gemini Enterprise" content for education, business, finance, and so on.
  - Conduct live training and/or webinars.
- **Recognize desired behaviors:**
  - Encourage your customer to think about ways to recognize behaviors they want to see happening and how to encourage others to emulate them.
  - Recognition of desired behaviors related to the change reinforces employee engagement, company culture, and continued sponsor support.
  - Recognition can include:.
  - Public shout-outs (for example, appreciation and recognition messages from leaders and peers).
  - Award certificates.
  - Sharing examples of best practices displayed by team members.
