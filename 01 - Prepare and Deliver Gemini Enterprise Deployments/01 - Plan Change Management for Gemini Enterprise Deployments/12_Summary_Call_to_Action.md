# Summary / Call to Action


## **Review of change management journey**

Change management isn't an isolated process, it begins within the sales cycle but continues into delivery.

The Gemini Enterprise change management methodology will help you to address the challenges around change management. The change journey to long-term success begins with preparation and is then followed by four key phases - Excite, Activate, Expand, and Transform.

Each phase is crucial for setting up your customers for success, so make sure you don’t discount early preparation and communication, or analyzing success metrics long after go-live.

## **Prepare**

Gemini Enterprise is a home for several solutions, including the built-in assistant, Agents, Tools, Data connections, and Notebooks. However it's important to understand how Gemini Enterprise fits into a bigger strategy and transformation. Developing a mature agentic deployment requires a series of engagements, but Gemini Enterprise offers a value-driven roadmap towards those advanced capabilities.

### **Design your solution offerings**

**Day 1 - Immediate value**

- Leverage an intuitive, chat-based experience to interact with the system.
- Quickly unlock insights from their documents and existing data.
- Design no-code agents, build solutions without needing deep technical expertise right from the start.

**Early Months - Simple agents**

- Build out simple agents, further increasing value.
- Securely build and govern agents, ensuring that they operate within organizational guidelines.
- Connect agents to existing work apps, embedding AI directly into current workflows.

**Ongoing - Advanced agents and connectors**

- Fully integrate AI into daily tools and workflows, making it an indispensable part of operations.
- Orchestrate multiple agents for complex tasks, tackling more sophisticated challenges.

### **Key takeaways for a successful engagement**

## **Excite**

The excite phase is where you engage with sponsors and analyze organizational needs.

### **Sponsorship and Engagement**

In this phase you identify the key stakeholders in the organization to perform certain roles to enable a successful delivery of Gemini Enterprise. There are some core responsibilities that the key stakeholders must own, and it is a good idea to make sure they are clear about their responsibilities from the start.

*Click each****+****button to expand the items and learn more.*

- **Executive Sponsor:**

  - The Executive Sponsor role is a critical decision-making role, and the one who authorizes the change to occur within the organization, ties it to business strategy, and mobilizes people to take action.

  - This individual sends the initial announcement, shares the vision, secures support and resources, socializes change, and addresses roadblocks.

  - To indicate that this is a business change, this person should ideally be a recognizable figure in the business, such as the CEO, or sit in the C-Suite, such as the COO.

  - No Executive Sponsor = No Go.

  - A clear executive sponsor and a defined problem statement are the #1 predictors of success.

- **Executive Committee:**

  - The executive committee is composed of business leaders and influencers providing department-specific support and leadership.

  - Solicit broad support from executives and leadership by recruiting a cross-functional executive committee that can provide feedback on impacts and messaging, identify and address roadblocks, communicate key messages to the team and address resistance early and often.

  - An executive committee plays a liaison role within the business relating to matters such as two-way team communications, leading by example, and thinking long term.

- **Early Adopters:**

  - Early Adopters are ambassadors for change within the business.

  - It's important to recruit the right people as Early Adopters, and in the right number.

  - They are typically people who will help scale and accelerate change efforts in cross-functional business groups, and are enthusiastic about the transformation and about gaining business value from it.

  - They do not need to be technical people; their role will be more about providing leadership on the ground in the business.

  - You should aim to recruit a range of people in any client organization to act as Early Adopters.

  - This involves proactively recruiting and engaging with them, and keeping them visible and engaged throughout the deployment process.

- **Communicators and Trainers:**

  - Communicators and Trainers can help to sustain innovation and change.

  - This is a group of people drawn from different business units who are the biggest supporters of change.

  - Communicators and Trainers keep the momentum going in the change process by facilitating transformation labs, sharing success stories across the organization, ensuring that leaders are engaged and lead by example.

  - They ensure employees have access to ongoing education, and measure user adoption and impact.

  - Communicators and Trainers also prepare for new feature launches, to refresh and maintain end-user resources for ongoing adoption and to support new hire learning initiatives.

- **Security and Networking:**

  - Security and networking will need to establish the foundational identity and organizational structure in Google Cloud, including setting up super admins, users, and groups, and creating the Google Cloud domain and organization root.

  - They may also need to configure Google Cloud's Workforce Identity Federation to securely connect the project stakeholder’s existing external identity provider (IdP) with Google Cloud.

  - You should bring Security in on Day 1.

  - Engage the customer's SecOps and Governance teams from the very first meeting to prevent last-minute blockers.

  - Ensure you have relevant security and networking personnel onboard from the start to ensure security and access to the Gemini Enterprise solution.

  - For an enterprise-wide rollout, the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) or equivalent should be engaged.

### **Organizational Analysis**

Organizational Analysis helps an organization to understand the impact of the change on end users and illustrates how this change is a positive transformation to the way their users work.

> [!NOTE]
> Organizational impacts can be positive, but they can also present challenges.

**Objection handling**

Whatever the reason for objections, remember to welcome them, as they provide an opportunity to better understand the position of the prospect and get more specific information about their current setup.

In fact, if a prospect has no objections, this can often be a sign of low commitment or engagement. So, when there are objections, they can be used to advance the sale by getting more specific information about the prospect’s current environment.

In addition to handling objections, there are a few common pitfalls you can encounter if you’re not careful.

- Thinking you can preempt or prevent objections.

- Minimizing the problem.

- Losing sight of the goal.

- Rushing straight to the answer.

### **Innovation matrix and rubric**

*Click each tab to learn more.*

- **Innovation matrix:**

  - A recommended method your customers can use to efficiently map Gemini Enterprise capabilities to business needs is the Innovation Matrix.

  - A simple innovation matrix just requires the customer to come up with four business priorities which can be mapped across a grid against the key capabilities of the new technology.

  - The task is then to think of 3-5 “sticky note” ideas for how you can solve the business needs with these capabilities.

- **Rubric:**

  - Your customer can develop a rubric to rate ideas by assigning points on a 1-5 scale based on categories like impact, feasibility, priority, team readiness, size of the project, and technical complexity.

  - Your customer can then select starting projects based on the rubric.

  - This can help your customer plan for their engagement to include some number of prototypes, such as exploring and iterating on up to 20 query ideas, up to 5 no-code agent ideas, and 1 ADK or Conversational agent (or save these for separately scoped engagements).

## **Activate**

Gemini Enterprise provides a lot of value as soon as the first data connectors are established, but the core capabilities are available straight out-of-the-box.

### **Lead with Day 1 value**

It's important to think ahead and plan for future engagements, but in your first engagement you still need to deliver value right away. You can build momentum and customer excitement by showcasing immediate, out-of-the-box capabilities from Day-1.

### **Data connectors**

- On day one, it is highly recommended to stick to Generally Available (GA) data connectors.
- If working with non-GA data connectors, be prepared to encounter technical issues that may require waiting for connector development or alternative data access patterns to overcome.
- Contact your Partner Development Manager (PDM) or Partner Advisor (PA) for an up-to-date list of data connectors and their statuses.
- Customers can also develop custom data connectors.

### **Pro-tips for data connectors**

- Validate everything first

- De-risk with a technical pre-flight check

- Start small

- Make sure your testers, including execs, have permissions

- Clearly define what 'good' looks like

- Leverage your Google resources

- Work with Partner Engineers and Google contacts to file bugs

- Don't assume that adding third-party data connectors will be easy

### **Develop starter no-code agents**

Start simple and get more complex as needed.

1. Identify some business needs that are strong candidates for an agentic solution.

1. Attempt to address them with a simple query or prompt.

1. If they can't be resolved through a simple query, try building a simple no-code agent.

1. If the business needs are still not satisfied, develop a more complex agent using Agent Development Kit (ADK) or Conversational Agents.

It's important for your customer to understand how easy it is to create a starter agent using Gemini Enterprise's no-code Agent Designer.

When writing instructions for agents, use best practice prompting strategies.

## **Expand**

A strong Communications strategy is important to raise awareness, build excitement, sell the benefits, and provide a feedback channel.

### **Communications**

It's recommended to take a two-fold, multi-directional approach where your customer simultaneously works top-down and bottom-up. This means combining high-level strategic planning (top-down) with practical input from teams who will use the technology (bottom-up).

*Click each****+****button to expand the items and learn more.*

- **Top-down:**

  - - Create a clear and compelling vision for AI by aligning AI solutions with strategic business priorities.

  - - Identify the most important use cases and workflows.

  - - Then, consider how AI can support those priorities.

- **Bottom-up:**

  - - Encourage teams to share their challenges and ideas for gen AI solutions.

  - - Encourage employees to use gen AI at a small scale.

  - Then share their solutions for broader implementation.

Successful Gemini Enterprise implementation requires a combination of top-down and bottom-up approaches, with two main levels of stakeholders for expanding the usage of Gemini Enterprise: **Executives and high-level management**, and **mid-level managers and individual contributors**.

For both approaches, it's a good idea to focus on:

- Strategic focus

- Exploration

- Responsible AI

- Resourcing

- Impact

- Continuous improvement

**Map out a Communications Plan**

A communications plan is essential for creating a way to distribute successful solutions and assets, initiate two-way communications channels, gather feedback and run events that harvest the potential of the user's imagination.

**Share use cases and success stories**

Gathering and sharing successes is key to building support. It's how people understand how this will benefit them. It also gives ideas and confidence to users about how to work in a new way.

## **Transform**

Training is important because it establishes 'why' you are implementing Gemini Enterprise, provides the skills for success, promotes engagement, and minimizes productivity issues. Analytics provide insight into the usage trends, search quality, and end-user engagement of your customers app.

### **Training**

A training analysis and plan should consist of the following:

- Outline training goals, objectives, and success criteria.
- Develop a training outline that includes all stages of the training.
- Plan logistical training considerations, such as:

- Instructor-led training, elearning, or both
- Group training, individual training, or both
- Pre-work training, on-the-job training, or both
- Training evaluation considerations.

### **Best practices**

Follow these best practices to finalize a training outline and plan:

*Click each****+****button to expand the items and learn more.*

- **Help users be productive on Day 1:**

  - Enable users to be productive from Day-1 with simple guidance on setup.

  - Focus on value (not features) by tying training to relevant practices or use cases.

- **Incorporate targeted training needed for special user groups:**

  - Address the user's needs in the training plan.

  - Identify advanced product features that would bring the most value to certain user groups, and plan training sessions to highlight these features.

- **Use existing training resources and formats:**

  - Work with the company’s training or HR department to understand how training is traditionally delivered in the organization.

  - If there is an online learning system and most users take eLearning courses, that may be a great option.

- **Ensure that the training format fits the content:**

  - Most users prefer to learn more complicated training from a live instructor so they can ask questions.

  - Conduct in-depth product training customized to the needs of each user group and using diversified training vehicles.

### **Analytics**

Gemini Enterprise uses user events to generate search-related metrics, such as search count and click-through-rate. No additional configurations are needed if your customer is using the user interface to search and get answers through the Gemini Enterprise assistant. However, if they use the Gemini Enterprise APIs in their apps, they must configure the search in the app to capture the user events and then import user events to the app.

Your customer can view search analytics for their app. They can view the metrics related to the search calls made to their app. If their app is connected to multiple data stores, then making search calls to the app by specifying the engine resource is recommended so that they can get results from across all its connected data stores.

There are four main metric categories through which to view analytics data, **Adoption**, **Usage and Quality**, **Agent**, and **Value**.

### **Key documents**

*Click each****+****button to expand the items and learn more.*

- **Sponsorship Assessment:**

  - The Sponsorship Assessment could be used to identify and categorize sponsors, capture sponsor demographic details, track the kickoff meeting status, and document sponsor concerns and an action plan.

  - It could also be used to outline the strategies, methods, and medium used, with recommendations of engagement for increasing sponsor commitment.

- **Organizational Analysis:**

  - An Organizational Analysis document could be used to provide details of the organizational impact for any Gemini Enterprise projects.

  - It can be helpful to identify the user groups affected, capture the current behavior or processes, identify the skills required for success, identify organizational policies or processes that need to be changed, and identify business processes and solutions that could be re-engineered and transformed using Gemini Enterprise.

- **Technical Design Document (TDD):**

  - The TDD is used to outline the technical specifications, architecture, and components for the Gemini Enterprise implementation.

- **Communications Plan:**

  - A Communications Plan could be used to define how your team, the customer, and other sponsors can most effectively communicate together.

  - This plan could set guidelines for how information will be shared, who's responsible for what, and who needs to be looped in on project communications.

  - Additionally, the plan could define the delivery method, intended audience, and delivery date of each message.

  - It might be helpful to include deployment notifications and strategic and marketing messages in the plan.

- **Training Needs Assessment:**

  - The Training Needs Assessment can be used to identify and outline the analysis of key job roles within the customer’s organization, the skills and knowledge customers will require, the courses required to achieve this, the course delivery methods, the course duration, the learning objectives, and the target audience.
