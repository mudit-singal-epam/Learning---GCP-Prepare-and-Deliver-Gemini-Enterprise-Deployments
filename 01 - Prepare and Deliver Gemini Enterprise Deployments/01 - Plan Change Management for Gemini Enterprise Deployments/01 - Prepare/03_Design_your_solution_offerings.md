# Design your solution offerings

## **Plan the phases of your engagements**

During an initial preparation of solution offerings, plan ahead for future engagements.

## **Gemini Enterprise is a home for several solutions**

Gemini Enterprise is an advanced agentic platform that brings the best of Google AI to every employee, for every workflow. It empowers teams to discover, create, share, and run AI agents—all in one secure environment.

Gemini Enterprise appears to users as a page that displays their recent files, calendar events, and other information they access regularly. It can make a great homepage from which to start daily work.

Some of the core solutions in Gemini Enterprise include:

- **Built-in Assistant:**

  - A powerful search bar, which functions like a Google search engine pointing at content to which the user has access.

  - It combines keyword search with generative-AI enabled search and an AI assistant to make it easy to retrieve information from an organization's online drives, common communication methods like email and chat, databases, ticketing systems, calendars, and more.

- **Agents:**

  - Agents are purpose-built to help your customer complete specific tasks.

  - They can use the ones provided or create their own! They can build no-code agents with the new visual agent builder,.

  - Conversational Agents simplifies how AI agents are built, leveraging the latest Gemini models and Agent Development Kit (ADK), with a no-code console.

  - They can also build more complex multi-agent frameworks for coordinating and performing specific tasks with Conversational Agents or ADK.

- **Tools and Data connections:**

  - Enhance conversations with Tools that provide extra generative AI functionality such as searching Google or internal sources, generating video or images, and running deep research.

  - Granting Gemini Enterprise users access to an organization’s data happens by adding one or more data stores.

  - Data stores allow users to easily connect to data with appropriate search, often with hybrid search combining basic keyword and AI-powered semantic search.

  - Many more connectors are available or under development for first and third party sources.

  - Explore other connectors, view their configuration requirements, and find links to their documentation by selecting them from connected data stores.

- **Notebooks:**

  - Add NotebookLM notebooks! Think of Notebook LM as a virtual note-taking and research assistant.

  - NotebookLM takes information, digests and analyzes it so that your customer can glean more from it.

  - It’s designed for that “deeper dive” your customer may need to take into a topic, or multiple topics at once.

  - Combine sources into one or more notebooks, get responses grounded in data with Gemini, and create audio overviews, briefing documents, and more.

## **Each solution offers opportunities for engagements**

Gemini Enterprise is a home for several solutions, including the built-in assistant, Agents, Tools, Data connections, and Notebooks. However it is just one part of a wider, more comprehensive deployment which consists of a series of engagements. A mature deployment of Gemini Enterprise may require several project engagements, such as identity and access management, connecting core data sources, networking, or training for built-in agents.

It's important to understand how Gemini Enterprise fits into a bigger strategy and transformation. Developing a mature agentic deployment requires a series of engagements, but Gemini Enterprise offers a value-driven roadmap towards those advanced capabilities.

- **Excite:**

  - The initial Excite phase focuses on identifying stakeholders, defining the vision, scope, and rollout strategy, setting up the adoption team, and deploying a minimum viable Gemini Enterprise solution.

- **Activate:**

  - The Activate phase begins leveraging chat, pre-built, and no-code agents to build user trust and adoption, supported by tight communication and user starter kits.

  - This phase is where the largest deployment rollout and user adoption occurs, so it is crucial to ensure the actions taken in this phase provide a strong foundation for the following phases.

  - From Day 1, your customer will experience immediate value.

  - They can leverage an intuitive, chat-based experience to interact with the system.

  - They can quickly unlock insights from their documents, gaining an immediate understanding from their existing data.

  - Gemini Enterprise also allows them to create and understand rich media, expanding the types of content they can work with.

  - And significantly, they can design their own no-code agents, empowering them to build solutions without needing deep technical expertise right from the start.

- **Expand:**

  - Moving into the early months, the Expand phase drives sustained engagement by integrating new data sources and more complex agents, while offering adoption activities to foster AI fluency.

  - Your customers investment allows them to build out simple agents, further increasing its value.

  - They can securely build and govern these agents, ensuring that they operate within organizational guidelines.

  - This phase also allows them to scale agents with confidence, expanding their reach and impact.

  - They will be able to find answers across enterprise data, centralizing and streamlining information access.

  - And importantly, they can connect these agents to existing work apps, embedding AI directly into current workflows.

- **Transform:**

  - The Transform phase aims to champion continuous innovation, scale output, and reimagine how work gets done, successfully transforming the workforce.

  - The value continues to grow into an ongoing phase, where your customer can develop advanced agents and connectors.

  - Here, they can fully integrate AI into daily tools and workflows, making it an indispensable part of their operations.

  - They can orchestrate multiple agents for complex tasks, tackling more sophisticated challenges.

  - This advanced capability allows them to create new revenue with AI, opening up new business opportunities.

  - Ultimately, Gemini Enterprise helps your customer to deliver personalized AI agents for all, tailoring AI solutions to individual needs across their organization.

## **Deploying Gemini Enterprise: Proven 3 stage migration approach**

The Activate phase is where Google or Partners support the deployment of services, the integration of enterprise data and third-party applications, building the first wave of low complexity and high usage agents, and helping ensure successful user adoption.

At the end of this phase, your customer will have configured core features and connectors, while experimenting with agents. They will have successfully helped their employees begin their Gemini Enterprise journey with starter kits and clear lines of communication, all while establishing a feedback loop for input.

This is achieved through a 3 stage approach over a period of weeks:

*Click each tab to learn more.*

- **Core IT:**

  - In this first stage, it's only the core IT technical teams who are involved, and they start by confirming and testing the technical design, identifying integration points, and becoming familiar with tools and technology.

  - Next, they can configure Gemini Enterprise features, connect enterprise data, and test the first wave of enterprise data with a small group of power users before a broader rollout.

  - They are also responsible for setting up and monitoring usage insights, so that they can track and share Gemini Enterprise adoption metrics across regions with sponsors and program leads.

  - The key participants would be:.

  - - Power Users.

  - - IT Administrators.

  - - Adoption Program Leads.

  - - Data Analysts.

- **Early Adopters:**

  - The next phase initially involves identifying and enabling the Early Adopters, who will be made up of only 5-10% of the organisation.

  - These early adopters will experiment with lower complexity agents, and integrate the first set of single-purpose agents, drawing from frequently encountered queries and use cases.

  - This enables validating the migration approach, testing the change management plan, and gathering feedback on training and communications.

  - The key participants would be:.

  - - AI Developers.

  - - Data Analysts.

  - - Early Adopters.

- **Global Go-Live:**

  - The final stage of the Activate phase is Global Go-Live, this involves bringing the remainder of organization onto the same system, conducting extensive training and communications, and transitioning to long term adoption and transformation efforts.

  - The key tasks for Global Go-Live include:.

  - **Distribute day one starter kits**.

  - Share persona-based starter kits with function specific use cases, sample prompt, and demo videos to encourage usage.

  - **Communicate and train**.

  - Establish an intentional communication cadence.

  - Design targeted training curriculum based role, function, and need.

  - **Setup office hours and establish feedback loop**.

  - Setup office hours and touch points to gather feedback.

  - Make it easy for users to report issues and make suggestions.

  - The key participants would be:.

  - - All End Users, Power Users,.

  - - Adoption Leads.

  - - Enablement.

  - - IT Help Desk.

## **Adoption for Gemini Enterprise**

Intentional adoption planning is important not only for the success of Gemini Enterprise, but because Google Cloud is the platform chosen by the customer to transform their organisation. Unlike generic SaaS adoption, there may not be two opportunities. The first wave is critical - and it is important not to stop there after the initial "go-live."

Additionally, because Gemini Enterprise’s capabilities will constantly improve —with new features, interface updates, and workflow enhancements regularly deployed—the adoption push needs to be a sustained process of ensuring users embrace these changes.

If users fail to adopt new functionalities or, worse, become disengaged due to the persistent updates, the perceived value of the platform plummets.

Following the launch of Gemini Enterprise, customer demand is focused on one question: "How do we successfully deploy this platform and drive adoption across our organization?"

Each phase has two critical, parallel tracks to ensure a holistic approach to customer success: Technical Enablement and User Empowerment.

- The **Technical Enablement** track focuses on all the core activities required to get the solution running correctly within the customer's environment. This includes platform configuration, integration with third-party systems, and security setup. Essentially, this track is about making the technology work.

- The **User Empowerment** track concentrates on the people-centric elements crucial for realizing business value. This includes change management, role-based training, workflow redesign, and communication strategies to ensure employees embrace and effectively utilize Gemini Enterprise in their daily work. This track is about making the technology work

## **Prioritize Identity and Access Configuration**

Configuring an identity provider and determining access patterns is an important step of a Gemini Enterprise deployment. Consider the customer’s needs according to their identity provider and the data connectors they will add to Gemini Enterprise in this engagement.

If your customer uses Google Identity and will only be accessing Google data sources, identity setup will be straightforward. If access should be granted according to a user’s identity, you may still want to [configure access controls for BigQuery and Cloud Storage data](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/identity?hl=en).

If your customer uses a third-party identity provider such as Microsoft Entra ID or Okta, you will need to [configure Workforce Identity Federation](https://cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/configure-identity-provider#workforce-id-fed-for-third-party-idps).

If your customer would like to add access to a third-party data connector, make sure you account for the steps required to [configure access to each third-party data connector](https://cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/connect-third-party-data-source).

## **Potential Risks and Mitigation**

| Risk category | Risk description | Potential mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Project Scoping and Onboarding** | Mismatch between project stakeholder expectations and out-of-the-box connector capabilities. | Conduct thorough discovery. Use a "safe questions" list and demos to clarify supported functionalities per connector. Clearly document out-of-the-box limitations against customization needs. Set realistic expectations early. |
| **Project Scoping and Onboarding** | Delays due to security considerations (such as connector permissions, data governance) not being addressed early in the project lifecycle. | Integrate a security workshop into the standard onboarding process. Proactively engage project stakeholder security teams to review requirements. |
| **Identity and Access Control** | Complex or misunderstood identity management requirements (for example WIF setup, third Party IDP limitations, ACL replication) lead to delays or errors. | Develop and share clear documentation or flowcharts for identity management scenarios (first or third party IDP, WIF). Ensure dedicated discussion on ACL strategy and feasibility per connector. |
| **Identity and Access Control** | Connectors for first-party Google sources (such as BigQuery, Google Cloud Storage) may require separate ACL info management, creating unexpected project stakeholder effort. | Clearly communicate this requirement during design. Plan for the work required to build and maintain ACL information if native IAM passthrough is not fully supported for the use case. |
| **Connector and Data Ingestion** | Project stakeholder security team raises concerns about the scope of permissions required by a connector, delaying or blocking its use. | Socialize connector permission requirements with project stakeholder security teams early. Be prepared to discuss permission rationale and alternatives (for example data migration to Google Cloud Storage, limiting scope). |
| **Connector and Data Ingestion** | Syncing on-premises data sources is significantly delayed by network connectivity, firewall rules, or private connectivity setup issues. | Rigorously verify all network prerequisites before starting. Allocate specific buffer time in the project plan for debugging private/on-premises connectivity. |
| **Connector and Data Ingestion** | Initial data sync for connectors with very large data volumes (such as extensive Slack history, large document repositories) takes excessively long. | Work with the project stakeholder to define a smaller, priority data subset for initial sync to speed up evaluation. Set clear expectations on full sync duration. Explore phased rollout if feasible. |
| **Connector and Data Ingestion** | Ingestion or indexing failures for specific file types or content exceeding limits (for example very large spreadsheets, high-resolution images). | Document known limitations on file sizes and types for internal reference and expectation setting. Advise project stakeholders on pre-processing or segmenting large content if necessary. |
| **Delivery and Dependencies** | Project stalls due to lack of timely project stakeholder responsiveness (such as providing system access, data for evaluation, ACL/IDP resolution). | Clearly define project stakeholder dependencies and required timelines in the project plan. Escalate risks of non-responsiveness to the project sponsor. Identify key project stakeholder contacts early. |
| **Delivery and Dependencies** | Significant project time is consumed by debugging connector sync or search quality issues due to limited error messages or logs. | Budget for debugging cycles. Familiarize the delivery team with available advanced debugging guides and tools. Foster a strong feedback loop with Product and Engineering for recurring or complex issues. |
| **Search Functionality** | Search results do not meet project stakeholder expectations for accuracy or relevance for specific connectors or query types. | Implement a robust evaluation framework (golden datasets). Iterate on system instructions, data chunking, and search controls. Clearly document any known limitations of search over certain data structures. |

## **Key takeaways for a successful engagement**

- Prioritize identity and security

  Address identity management (especially for third Party IDPs) and connector security permissions with the project stakeholder in the first week. These are the most common sources of significant delays.
- Conduct Day-1 value workshop

  Successful completion of the Day 1 value experience so as to socialize the features to a broader audience.
- Set connector expectations early

  Use the provided guidance on connectors to set realistic expectations about what each connector can and cannot do out-of-the-box. Not all connectors support granular ACLs or natural language queries on day one.
- Validate on-premises connectivity immediately

  If connecting to on-premises data sources, treat the network and connectivity validation as a critical path item. This process can often take longer than expected.
- Budget for debugging and evaluation

  Build explicit time into the project plan for debugging data sync issues and for a multi-stage (automated and manual) evaluation of search results. Rushing this step will impact quality.
